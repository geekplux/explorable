Why coffee cools fast, then slowly

A hot drink loses heat in proportion to how much hotter it is than the room. That one rule makes the gap shrink by the same fraction every minute, so it always takes the same time to halve.

Based on Newton's law of cooling

Heat flows down the gap

A cup at 90 °C in a 20 °C room loses heat quickly. At 25 °C it barely cools. Newton's law of cooling makes this exact: the cup cools at a rate proportional to the gap between its temperature and the room's.

dT/dt = −k·(T − Tₐ)

The temperature T changes at a rate of −k times the gap to the room temperature Tₐ: the bigger the gap, the faster the fall.

So the hotter the cup is compared with the room, the faster it cools. As it cools, the gap shrinks, and so does the rate. That feedback is the whole story.

The curve

Follow that rule minute by minute and the gap shrinks by the same fraction each minute, so the temperature falls steeply at first and then levels off. Drag the point along time and watch the drop slow down. Raise k, the cooling constant, and the whole curve falls faster toward the dotted room-temperature line.

T(t) = Tₐ + (T₀ − Tₐ)·e−kt

After t minutes the cup is at the room temperature plus what is left of the starting gap, which shrinks by the same fraction every minute; k sets how fast.

Now watch the labelled marker line while you change T₀, the starting temperature. The curve moves; the marker does not. The time for the gap to halve depends on k alone.

A half-life for the gap

Because the gap always shrinks by the same fraction, it takes the same time to halve, whatever the starting temperature. A cup at 90 °C and one at 50 °C halve their gaps in the same time: 70 degrees become 35, and 30 become 15.

t½ = ln 2 / k

The gap halves every 0.69 divided by k minutes: 13.9 minutes at k = 0.05 per minute, from any starting temperature.

Drag k along the curve below. A slow cooler, k = 0.01 per minute, needs about 69 minutes per halving; a fast one, k = 0.3 per minute, about 2 minutes. Double k and the half-life halves, so the curve falls steeply and then flattens.

Takeaway

Cooling is proportional to the gap, so the gap shrinks by the same fraction every minute. The temperature falls fast, then slowly, and the gap halves in the same time from any start.
